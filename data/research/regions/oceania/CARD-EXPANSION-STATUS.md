## Identity and chronology decisions

- Samoa describes the western islands' colonial and independent state history, with older archaeological background identified separately. American Samoa has its own card, cession documents and continuing territorial relationship.
- Fiji's 1874 cession and 1970 independence are distinct from the map's 1878 first observation. Diaspora examples identify their later setting.
- Tonga's 1875 parliamentary opening is not represented as the constitution's exact promulgation day. Its 1900 to 1970 protected-state relationship is distinguished from Fiji's colonial administration.
- The later Tu'i Tonga record distinguishes sacred rank, changing governing lines and the last titleholder's death in 1865. The early chronology is explicitly attributed to recorded oral tradition, while the Heketa archaeological interpretation is attributed separately. The imported `tu-i-tonga-empire` record at 500 to 700 remains unresolved and has not been copied from the later card.
- These five records keep their available map coverage as coverage. New researched phases do not certify imported boundaries. No unverified successor navigation has been added.
- American Samoa's Navy-to-Interior transfer is dated from Executive Order 10264 to 1 July 1951. This takes precedence over a conflicting 1956 date on a general Interior overview page. The 1967 constitution is described as revised, supported by the territorial commission report.
