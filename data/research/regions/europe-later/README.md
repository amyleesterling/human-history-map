## Integration rules

- QWEN owns Asia. Transcontinental identities and cross-border successor links need coordination. The Swedish Chinese Pavilion item describes reception within Sweden, not the origin of Asian art.
- The chapter dates fit the current merged polity intervals. That is a compatibility check, not independent verification of the intervals.
- The imported names frequently combine different constitutions or split historical continuities. England-and-Ireland is particularly unsuitable as an unqualified civilization identity. Its overview identifies the geographic limits of this chapter.
- Florence's existing record begins too late for the Florentine Republic's Renaissance story. Stage an identity correction rather than attaching earlier achievements to that record.
- The current Netherlands record beginning in 1715 cannot represent the entire seventeenth-century Dutch Republic. A separate identity review comes before a Golden Age chapter.
- No geometry was researched or changed here. UNESCO heritage boundaries describe properties, not state sovereignty.
- No endpoint was invented to fill a card. Sourced political transitions and destination IDs remain a required next pass. An imported snapshot endpoint is not evidence that a society fell then.
- Court art and surviving monuments overrepresent wealthy patrons. This batch must be extended with evidence about ordinary work, women, minorities, rural households and coercion before being called thorough.

## Source cautions

The University of Copenhagen's Bohr career landing page contains an obvious inauguration-year typo and a misleading national Nobel-priority statement. The cards use the detailed institute history and the narrowly relevant quantum discussion instead.

The Polish diplomatic-history page has errors outside the selected claim, including partition dates. Only its narrow Union of Lublin formation statement is used. Do not reuse that page as an unchecked chronology or adopt its universal-priority claims.

UNESCO summaries can contain superlatives, nationalist framing and imprecise medieval foundation narratives. This batch uses concrete construction, institutional and material facts and avoids claims of universal invention or priority. Genoa's two sources are institutionally related and are not independent corroboration.
