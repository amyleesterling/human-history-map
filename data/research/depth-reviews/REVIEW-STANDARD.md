# Historical depth review

Review the exact proposed card and record its SHA-256 after corrections.
Approval of prose does not approve imported borders or inferred lifetimes.

1. Read cited excavation reports, object catalogues or scholarship directly.
2. Identify each object's actual findspot and archaeological period. A museum
   object from another site must not become a find at the featured capital.
3. Distinguish a physical observation from an interpretation of its use,
   meaning or social consequences. Preserve the author's uncertainty.
4. Separate settlement, political state, archaeological culture and living
   people. A predecessor settlement is not a state's founding date.
5. State an ending mechanism when supported, including conquest, dissolution,
   political relocation or gradual decline. Continuing peoples do not receive
   an invented fall date. Unknown mechanisms remain explicitly unknown.
6. Check the successor's identity at the navigation year. Approximate dates
   remain visibly approximate; a link year is not independent historical proof.
7. Review period cards for retrospective leakage. Distinguish when an object
   was made from when archaeologists excavated it.
8. Check numerical precision and the total paraphrase attributable to each
   source, including overview, period text, sections and ending together.
9. Record publication blockers separately from limitations that honest wording
   can explain. Re-read changed claims before clearing blockers.

This is a claim review, not a claim that all research on a society is complete.
